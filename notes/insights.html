Design

Lean Startup in practice

Instead of designing a full productivity suite up front, each insight started as the smallest version that could be useful. It shipped to real users with usage instrumented from day one. The numbers decided what happened next: insights people came back to were deepened, and the ones they ignored were retired. The product grew from evidence rather than from a roadmap of guesses.

Insights as independent modules

That way of working needs an architecture where adding or removing an insight is cheap. Each insight is a small module that takes its source data and returns a typed result. The desktop client renders any result that matches the shared contract as a card. A new insight never touches the others, which is what made fast experiments practical.

Migrating the core to TypeScript

To expand firm-wide, the core insights moved from .NET to TypeScript. That put the insight logic in the same language as the Electron client and its typed contracts, so one team could change an insight end to end and the compiler would catch contract mismatches across the boundary. The migration could go insight by insight behind the same contract, which kept the app working throughout.

Rolling out to 350K+ employees

Going from early adopters to the whole firm changes the priorities from feature speed to reliability and consistency. Proven insights, a single language and typed contracts made that step manageable. The app now reaches more than 350,000 employees.
